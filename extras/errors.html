Common Math Errors

These are errors that appear repeatedly in student work. Review them carefully — being aware of them is the first step to avoiding them.

Exponent Errors

Error 1 — Distributing Exponents Over Addition
$(a + b)^n \neq a^n + b^n$ in general. For example: $$(x + 3)^2 \neq x^2 + 9 \qquad \text{Correct: } (x+3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$$ Exponents distribute over multiplication, not addition.
Error 2 — Cancelling Terms in Fractions
You can only cancel factors (things multiplied), not terms (things added): $$\frac{x^2 + 4}{x} \neq x + 4 \qquad \text{but} \qquad \frac{x^2 \cdot 4}{x} = 4x \; \checkmark$$

Radical Errors

Error 3 — Square Root of a Sum
$$\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \neq a + b \qquad \text{e.g. } \sqrt{9+16} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \neq 3+4$$
Error 4 — Forgetting the $\pm$ When Taking Square Roots
Solving $x^2 = 9$ gives $x = \pm 3$, not just $x = 3$. Both solutions must be checked.

Fraction / Rational Expression Errors

Error 5 — Adding Fractions with Different Denominators
$$\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} \neq \frac{1}{a+b} \qquad \text{Correct: } \frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b} = \frac{a+b}{ab}$$
Error 6 — Dividing Out the Wrong Way
$$\frac{a+b}{b} \neq a \qquad \text{Correct: } \frac{a+b}{b} = \frac{a}{b} + 1$$

Equation-Solving Errors

Error 7 — Not Checking for Extraneous Solutions
When solving equations involving radicals, absolute values, or logarithms, always check your solutions in the original equation. Some solutions introduced by squaring or other manipulations will not satisfy the original.
Error 8 — Dividing Both Sides by a Variable
Never divide both sides of an equation by a variable expression unless you are sure it is nonzero. Doing so can lose solutions. Example: from $x^2 = 3x$, dividing by $x$ gives $x = 3$ and loses the solution $x = 0$.

Logarithm Errors

Error 9 — Log of a Sum
$$\log(a + b) \neq \log a + \log b \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{\log a}{\log b} \neq \log\!\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)$$ The product and quotient rules apply to $\log(ab)$ and $\log(a/b)$ only.

Trigonometry Errors

Error 10 — $\sin^{-1}x$ vs. $\frac{1}{\sin x}$
$\sin^{-1}x$ means the inverse sine function (arcsin), not $\dfrac{1}{\sin x}$ (which is $\csc x$). The notation is unfortunately ambiguous — context and surrounding text determine the meaning.
Error 11 — Wrong Sign in the Double-Angle Formula for Cosine
There are three equivalent forms of $\cos 2\theta$. The most error-prone step is picking the wrong one: $$\cos 2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$$ All three are correct, but mixing them up (e.g., writing $2\cos^2\theta + 1$) is a frequent mistake.
