Chapter 1: Exponents & Radicals

1.1 — Integer Exponents

If $a$ is any real number and $n$ is a positive integer, we define $$a^n = \underbrace{a \cdot a \cdot a \cdots a}_{n \text{ factors}}$$ We call $a$ the base and $n$ the exponent (or power).

Definition — Zero and Negative Exponents
For any nonzero $a$: $$a^0 = 1 \qquad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$$ Note: $0^0$ is undefined (or indeterminate, depending on context).
Theorem — Laws of Exponents
Let $a, b$ be real numbers and $m, n$ be integers (with bases nonzero where needed):
LawExample
$a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$$x^3 \cdot x^5 = x^8$
$\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$$\dfrac{x^7}{x^2} = x^5$
$(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$(x^3)^4 = x^{12}$
$(ab)^n = a^n b^n$$(2x)^3 = 8x^3$
$\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$$\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{x^2}{9}$
$a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$$x^{-4} = \dfrac{1}{x^4}$
Common Mistake
$(a + b)^n \neq a^n + b^n$ in general. For example, $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$, not $x^2 + 9$. The exponent does not distribute over addition.

Worked Example 1.1

Simplify $\dfrac{(2x^3 y^{-2})^4}{4x^5 y^3}$, writing your answer with positive exponents only.

Solution:

First expand the numerator using $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$:

$$\frac{(2x^3 y^{-2})^4}{4x^5 y^3} = \frac{2^4 \cdot x^{12} \cdot y^{-8}}{4x^5 y^3} = \frac{16 x^{12} y^{-8}}{4 x^5 y^3}$$

Now divide coefficients and apply $a^m / a^n = a^{m-n}$:

$$= 4 \cdot x^{12-5} \cdot y^{-8-3} = 4x^7 y^{-11} = \frac{4x^7}{y^{11}}$$

1.2 — Radicals and Rational Exponents

Definition — $n$th Root
For a positive integer $n$ and real number $a$, the principal $n$th root of $a$ is $$\sqrt[n]{a} = a^{1/n}$$ meaning the non-negative number $b$ such that $b^n = a$ (when $a \geq 0$). More generally, $a^{m/n} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$.
Properties of Radicals
$$\sqrt[n]{ab} = \sqrt[n]{a}\,\sqrt[n]{b} \qquad \sqrt[n]{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}} \qquad \sqrt[m]{\sqrt[n]{a}} = \sqrt[mn]{a}$$

A radical expression is in simplified form when: (1) no perfect $n$th-power factors remain under the radical, (2) no fractions appear under the radical, and (3) no radicals appear in a denominator (rationalized).

Worked Example 1.2

Simplify $\sqrt{72x^5 y^8}$.

Solution: Factor out perfect squares:

$$\sqrt{72 x^5 y^8} = \sqrt{36 \cdot 2 \cdot x^4 \cdot x \cdot y^8} = 6x^2 y^4 \sqrt{2x}$$

Worked Example 1.3

Rationalize the denominator: $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2}}$.

Solution: Multiply by the conjugate $(\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{2})$:

$$\frac{3}{\sqrt{5}-\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{2}} = \frac{3(\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{2})}{5 - 2} = \frac{3(\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{2})}{3} = \sqrt{5}+\sqrt{2}$$
Note — Rational Exponent Connection
Converting between radical and exponent notation is often useful for simplification: $\sqrt[3]{x^2} = x^{2/3}$, so $\left(\sqrt[3]{x^2}\right)^6 = x^{(2/3)\cdot 6} = x^4$.

Practice Problems

Solutions:
Problem 1
Simplify: $\left(\dfrac{3a^{-2}b^3}{a^4 b^{-1}}\right)^2$. Write your answer with positive exponents.
Solution

First simplify inside the parentheses:

$$\frac{3a^{-2}b^3}{a^4 b^{-1}} = 3 \cdot a^{-2-4} \cdot b^{3-(-1)} = 3a^{-6}b^4$$

Then square:

$$(3a^{-6}b^4)^2 = 9a^{-12}b^8 = \frac{9b^8}{a^{12}}$$
Problem 2
Simplify: $\sqrt{50x^3 y^6}$
Solution

Factor out the largest perfect square from each part:

$$\sqrt{50x^3y^6} = \sqrt{25 \cdot 2 \cdot x^2 \cdot x \cdot y^6} = 5xy^3\sqrt{2x}$$
Problem 3
Rationalize the denominator and simplify: $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{7}+\sqrt{3}}$
Solution

Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate $(\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{3})$:

$$\frac{4}{\sqrt{7}+\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{3}} = \frac{4(\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{3})}{7-3} = \frac{4(\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{3})}{4} = \sqrt{7}-\sqrt{3}$$
Problem 4
Rewrite $\sqrt[4]{x^3}$ as a power of $x$, then evaluate $\left(\sqrt[4]{x^3}\right)^8$ in simplified form.
Solution

$\sqrt[4]{x^3} = x^{3/4}$

Then:

$$\left(x^{3/4}\right)^8 = x^{(3/4)\cdot 8} = x^6$$
Problem 5
Simplify completely: $\dfrac{x^{3/2} \cdot x^{1/3}}{x^{5/6}}$
Solution

Add exponents in the numerator (find common denominator 6):

$$x^{3/2} \cdot x^{1/3} = x^{9/6} \cdot x^{2/6} = x^{11/6}$$

Then divide:

$$\frac{x^{11/6}}{x^{5/6}} = x^{11/6 - 5/6} = x^{6/6} = x$$
Problem 6
Evaluate without a calculator: $\left(\dfrac{8}{27}\right)^{-2/3}$
Solution

Apply the negative exponent to flip the fraction, then the $2/3$ power:

$$\left(\frac{8}{27}\right)^{-2/3} = \left(\frac{27}{8}\right)^{2/3} = \left(\sqrt[3]{\frac{27}{8}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{4}$$
